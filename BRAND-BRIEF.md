# BRAND BRIEF — Music City Specialty Welding

## Snapshot
Music City Specialty Welding is a 24/7 welding and fabrication shop serving Greater Nashville and Middle Tennessee from 533 W Baddour Pkwy, Lebanon, TN. The work spans mobile and shop welding, architectural fabrication, trailer and equipment repair, aluminum and boat welding, specialty fabrication, custom wrought-iron mailboxes, and metal planter boxes for homeowners, contractors, facilities, utilities, municipalities, fleets, and industrial customers. The existing site is functional and currently supports proven Google Ads traffic, but its cream editorial styling, polite copy, and generic service-page rhythm do not sound or feel like the loud, Southern, blue-collar shop behind the work. This redesign must preserve ad recognition and all verified lead paths while turning the landing page into a distinctive brand and recruiting signal.

## Customer
The caller could be a homeowner staring at a broken gate, a contractor whose schedule just stopped, a facility or fleet manager with equipment down, or a project lead who needs serious fabrication delivered cleanly. They are usually on a phone, short on time, and trying to decide whether this shop can understand the problem without a long explanation, handle work at the required scale, and finish without excuses. They want the immediate feeling: “These guys get it.” The site must make small local jobs feel welcome without making commercial and industrial buyers think the shop is small-time.

## Character Statement
Music City Specialty Welding walks in wearing a black work shirt with burned cuffs, drives something built to work, talks with a Tennessee twang, and would rather show the weld than explain how good it is. The shop is loud, capable, legitimate, relentless, and unapologetically badass—but never fake-tough, sloppy, or too proud to sweat the details. It would never hide behind corporate filler, cowboy cosplay, or borrowed proof.

## Archetype
**RUGGED OUTLAW × BOLD EDITORIAL.** Rugged Outlaw is the skeleton: blunt voice, heavy type, carbon-dark surfaces, real work, and hard-edged motion. Bold Editorial supplies the ambition: oversized type, unexpected image choreography, art-directed pacing, and one unmistakable signature moment.

Deliberate deviations:

1. More commercial precision than a pure Outlaw brand: disciplined grids, legible specifications, and clean hierarchy keep large buyers confident.
2. Southern without costume: Tennessee rhythm and plain speech, never saloon fonts, flags everywhere, faux rust, or cartoon cowboy cues.
3. Recruitment is a secondary brand outcome: the site should make welders want to work here while keeping calls unmistakably primary.

## Voice
- Short sentences. Hard stops.
- Say “we.” Never “our team.”
- Name the metal, job, and place.
- Southern rhythm. Zero cowboy cosplay.
- Proof first. Swagger second.
- Plain words beat sales language.
- One sharp line per section.

Sample headlines:

1. **METAL PROBLEM? WE GET IT.**
2. **BUILT HERE. FIXED WHERE IT SITS.**
3. **BIG IRON. SMALL DETAILS. DONE RIGHT.**

Banned words and phrases: “quality you can trust,” “complete solutions,” “your one-stop shop,” “customer satisfaction is our priority,” “cutting-edge,” “exceptional service,” “premier provider,” “no job too big or too small,” “dependable workmanship,” “professional solutions,” “industry-leading,” and any sentence that says “we are committed to.”

## Palette
- **72% Carbon — `#0B0A09`:** primary canvas, navigation, large brand fields.
- **20% Bone — `#E8E1D4`:** primary light text, warm paper sections, form surfaces.
- **6% Steel — `#777872`:** rules, secondary text, stamped metadata, disabled states.
- **2% Arc — `#FF4A1C`:** the single hot accent for call actions, focus, active states, and rare visual sparks.

The existing logo’s blue and red remain inside the mark for recognition; they do not become competing page accents. Verified text pairs: Bone on Carbon and Carbon on Bone exceed normal-text contrast; Carbon on Arc is the preferred button-label pair. Steel is not used for small text on Carbon without a lighter token.

## Typography
**Display:** Archivo variable, `700–900`, width tightened for large all-caps statements. It is industrial and forceful without collapsing into a generic stencil or western costume.

**Body:** IBM Plex Sans, `400–600`, sentence case, open spacing, tuned for one-handed reading. Small labels use IBM Plex Mono `500–600` where a stamped job-ticket tone is useful.

Fluid type uses `clamp()` with breakpoint-specific line breaks, not automatic shrinkage. Headlines are all caps when declaring; body copy is sentence case. No long italic display runs, no hairline weights, and no paragraph narrower than comfortable phone reading.

## Motion Personality
**7/10 — kinetic and loud, never slow or floaty.** The site should feel like equipment engaging: immediate, mechanical, controlled.

Signature moves:

1. Headlines reveal through hard horizontal masks, like steel passing a cut line.
2. Image transitions snap between full-bleed work and tight detail crops; no soft cross-fade carousel.
3. A single “WE GET IT” sequence uses type, project imagery, and measured scroll choreography as the brand-only moment.

Motion must stop cleanly, never delay a call action, honor `prefers-reduced-motion`, and preserve a fast mobile load.
